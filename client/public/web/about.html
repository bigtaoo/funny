Free to play · Drawn on graph paper

The war for the notebook
runs in real time.

Nivara — also known as Notebook Wars — is a hand-drawn real-time strategy game fought in the margins of a school notebook. Gold refills on its own, your hand of cards keeps cycling, and both castles are always in reach: you decide what to spend, which lane to push, and when to hold.

What you play

Three ways to play, one shared roster of heroes and cosmetics.

Story campaign

A single-player journey through hand-drawn chapters, following the duel between Tao's and Anna's notebooks. Learn the lane combat, unlock heroes, and take the story to the last page.

Ranked duels

Real-time head-to-head matches, five to ten minutes each. Both clients simulate the same battle from the same inputs, so what you see is what your opponent sees. Climb a seasonal ladder for ranking rewards.

Open world

A large-scale strategy layer on a shared, changing map: claim ground, march on rivals, build a home city, and form alliances that hold territory together across a season.

Heroes and cosmetics

Collect and equip a roster of characters, dress them in stationery-themed skins, and gear them with equipment you earn. Progression never changes the rules of a match.

How it stays fair

The parts of a free-to-play game that usually go unsaid, said out loud.

  • Battles are server-authoritative and deterministic. Matches sync inputs rather than outcomes, and every result can be replayed from the same seed.
  • The game contains paid random items. Every draw rate and every pity guarantee is published inside the game, on its own odds page, before you spend anything.
  • Collecting and levelling changes what you bring to a match, not the rules of the match. There is no paid stat that a free player cannot reach.
  • Nothing is required to finish the campaign or to climb the ladder. The game is free to play, all the way through.
